II · Origin

The Glacier.

At 2,847 metres, in the Valais Alps, ice bears no language. It keeps calendar by pressure, by compression, by the slow migration of minerals through crystal lattice. What we bottle is not produced. It is translated.

Aletsch is Europe's longest glacier — 23 km of compressed snowfall, some layers still breathing air from before the agricultural revolution. We do not drill. We do not dynamite. We draw, quietly, from a single seasonal melt-fissure opened by the sun itself.

Field Note · 07:14 CET The mountain does the work. We carry weight, we carry patience, we carry nothing that cannot be folded back into stone. At 2,847 metres the air is thin enough to count syllables. We do not count.

IX · Ritual

Serve, slowly.

The oldest thing you'll drink today asks for very little. Two hands, one glass, no ice. It will keep whatever time you give it.

01

Temperature

Serve at 8 °C. Not colder. Cold mutes the mineral voice; warmth rushes it. 8 °C is the room Aletsch left open.

02

Vessel

Uncoloured crystal. Anything coloured overwrites the reading. Avoid plastic; avoid metal; avoid perfume on the rim.

03

Ice

None. Ice is a second water, with its own opinions. It will always win an argument you didn't start.

04

Pace

Small, unhurried sips. The finish is longer than you expect. Give it room. Give it silence, if you can.

05

Pair

Dry cheese, cured fish, stone fruit, or nothing at all. Nothing at all is the honest default.

Abstract. This lot documents a single seasonal draw from the Aletsch Glacier¹, Valais, at 2,847 m of altitude. The ice layer of origin is approximately 12,000 years old, carrying a δ¹⁸O signature of −17.2‰ consistent with late Pleistocene alpine accumulation.

cross-checked with the 1953 cantonal survey · slight drift in Mg, within tolerance

Water was gathered by gravity siphon, without pumping or thermal treatment, decanted in uncoated crystal at 8 °C, and bottled under nitrogen headspace² into a hexagonal cylindrical vessel of 6 mm crystal wall. Total Dissolved Solids measured at 145 mg/L; bicarbonate buffer 102 mg/L; pH 7.4.

All SKUs (330 mL · 750 mL · 1.5 L) share the same lot identifier. Each closure is individually etched. Each bottle is a single citation — of a mountain, of a millennium, of a very patient calendar.
